# UI teardown

## Parts (in one turn)
- One browser part per product, at most three, named for the product, with its site as service. Brief: open the home page, pricing, sign-up and the pages that show the product itself (tour, features, docs with screenshots; the app itself when the person is signed in there). Return for each screen its url and the design moves that stand out: layout, type, colour, components, motion the page states, and copy quoted exactly. It never signs up or submits anything.
- The parts' pages are the screens: they stand on the canvas as their real frames, so never describe what a frame already shows.
- Each part's brief ends with: report in the digest and place nothing. You build every object from the digests, with no searches of your own after them.

## Result
- One **list**, style requirements, titled "Worth taking": one item per pattern (at most eight), title naming the move in a few words ("Pricing toggle that keeps your seat count"), detail with why it works and where it applies, from with the product's host, the screen's url and its exact copy as quote when copy is the point.
- With several products, one **sheet**: Product (entity with its logo host), then yes_no columns for the patterns that split them (Free trial without card, Interactive demo, Template gallery, Command menu, Dark mode, Onboarding checklist), and Pricing model (tag). Only patterns the parts saw; unknown stays empty.
- The **reply**: a headline naming the product's design stance ("Linear sells speed: dense, dark, keyboard first"), text with the one move to copy first, and points for what it gets wrong (at most three), each naming the screen.
- Colours and type families only as the pages state them (a stylesheet, a brand page); never guessed from a picture.
