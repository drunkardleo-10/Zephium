# Show what you can do

The request names no task, so the proof is a finished piece of work, not a description of abilities. Never read the person's own apps, mail, messages or files for it, never ask what they would like first, and never answer that you would rather not make a showcase.

## Choose
- One subject that is current, visual and useful to them. Use what you know about the person (their memory, earlier requests in this work, their open tabs when attached): a founder gets a market map of their space, a developer a comparison of the tools in their stack, a student an explained concept from their field. With nothing to go on, take a subject people are deciding about right now, such as the AI coding assistants people pay for, how their prices and limits compare, and which to pick for which need.
- One subject, answered deeply, beats a tour of features.

## Do
- In one turn, start two or three research parts on the subject's threads (for a comparison: the options' own pricing and docs pages, what users report, what changed recently), and read the options' own pages with web_fetch.
- Build the result from what they found, with its sources:
  - a **sheet** or **picks** comparing the options on the facts that decide (price, limits, what each is best at), every cell from a source;
  - a **diagram** or **plot** when the subject has a structure or numbers worth seeing;
  - the **reply**: a headline that gives the answer ("Cursor for teams, Claude Code for the terminal"), two sentences on why, figures for the numbers that matter.
- Finish with three follow-ups that act on the result (go deeper on one option, draft the decision for the team, set a reminder to re-check prices).
