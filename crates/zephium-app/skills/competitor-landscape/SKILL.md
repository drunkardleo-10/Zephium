# Competitor landscape

## First
- Know the product and its market. When the request names only a category, the product is the person's own idea; when it names nothing to compare, find the five to eight players that matter.

## Parts (in one turn)
- **Players** — research part: who competes (the named ones, or the leaders and the fastest-growing newcomers from recent sources), each with founding year, funding and size when published, and the audience it serves.
- **Pitch** — research part: each competitor's own home and pricing pages by their plain address: the hero headline verbatim, the category they claim, the entry price and the free plan.
- Each part's brief ends with: report in the digest and place nothing. You build every object from the digests, with no searches of your own after them.

## Result
- One **sheet**, the landscape: Company (entity with its logo host), Positioning (text, a phrase in your words), For (tag: the audience), Starts at (money with currency, per month), Free plan (yes_no), two to four yes_no columns for the capabilities that split the market, Raised (money) when published. best min on Starts at. Unknown stays empty.
- One **list**, style reading, titled "How they pitch": one item per competitor, title its hero headline exactly as the page shows it, from with its host, the page url and the headline as quote.
- The **reply**: a headline naming the opening ("Everyone sells speed; nobody sells calm"), text with the one positioning move it suggests, points for up to three messages to test, each tied to a row of the sheet.
- No picks, no chart unless the person asks for one; no document of profiles.
